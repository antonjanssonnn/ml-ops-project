Honeybees are flying insects known for their role in pollination and for producing honey and beeswax. A honeybee colony is organized into three types of adult bees: a single queen, who is the only reproductive female and can lay up to 2,000 eggs per day during peak season; female worker bees, who forage for nectar and pollen, build and defend the hive, and care for larvae; and male drones, whose only role is to mate with a queen from another colony. Honeybees communicate the location of food sources to each other through a movement pattern known as the waggle dance, discovered and decoded by biologist Karl von Frisch, who won a Nobel Prize for the work in 1973. A single worker bee produces only about one twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in its lifetime, which typically lasts five to six weeks during the active summer months.
