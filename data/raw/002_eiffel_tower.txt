The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. It was designed by the engineer Gustave Eiffel's company and built between 1887 and 1889 as the entrance arch for the 1889 World's Fair, held to celebrate the centennial of the French Revolution. The tower stands 330 metres tall, roughly the same height as an 81-storey building, and was the tallest man-made structure in the world for 41 years, until the Chrysler Building in New York City was completed in 1930. It was initially criticized by some of France's leading artists and intellectuals for its design, but it has become a global cultural icon of France and one of the most recognizable structures in the world. Around 7 million people visit the Eiffel Tower each year.
