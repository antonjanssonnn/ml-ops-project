The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications built along the historical northern borders of China to protect against invasions and raids from various nomadic groups. Construction began as early as the 7th century BC, with earlier walls built by separate states, but the most famous sections were built during the Ming dynasty between 1368 and 1644. The wall stretches for approximately 21,000 kilometres in total, including all its branches, making it one of the longest structures ever built. It is constructed from a variety of materials including rammed earth, brick, stone, and wood, depending on the terrain and the era of construction. Contrary to a popular myth, the Great Wall is not visible to the naked eye from the Moon. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.
